1. k-means by hand

We cluster the four points $$x^{(1)}=(0,0),\quad x^{(2)}=(0,2),\quad x^{(3)}=(4,0),\quad x^{(4)}=(4,2)$$ into $k=2$ clusters. The distortion is $J(c,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^n\|x^{(i)}-\mu_{c^{(i)}}\|^2$.
(a) Initialize $\mu_1=x^{(1)}$ and $\mu_2=x^{(3)}$. Run $k$-means until the assignments no longer change. Give the centroids and $J$.
Solution. Assignment step. The squared distances $\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2$ are $$\begin{array}{c|cccc}&x^{(1)}&x^{(2)}&x^{(3)}&x^{(4)}\\\hline\mu_1=(0,0)&0&4&16&20\\ \mu_2=(4,0)&16&20&0&4\end{array}$$ so $c=(1,1,2,2)$.
Centroid step. $$\mu_1=\tfrac12\big((0,0)+(0,2)\big)=(0,1),\qquad\mu_2=\tfrac12\big((4,0)+(4,2)\big)=(4,1).$$
Assignment step. Every point is at squared distance $1$ from its own centroid and $17$ from the other, so $c$ does not change and $k$-means stops with $$\mu_1=(0,1),\qquad\mu_2=(4,1),\qquad J=1+1+1+1=4.$$
(b) Now initialize $\mu_1=x^{(1)}$ and $\mu_2=x^{(2)}$ and run $k$-means again.
Solution. Assignment step. $$\begin{array}{c|cccc}&x^{(1)}&x^{(2)}&x^{(3)}&x^{(4)}\\\hline\mu_1=(0,0)&0&4&16&20\\ \mu_2=(0,2)&4&0&20&16\end{array}$$ so $c=(1,2,1,2)$.
Centroid step. $$\mu_1=\tfrac12\big((0,0)+(4,0)\big)=(2,0),\qquad\mu_2=\tfrac12\big((0,2)+(4,2)\big)=(2,2).$$
Assignment step. Every point is at squared distance $4$ from its own centroid and $8$ from the other, so $c$ does not change and $k$-means stops with $$\mu_1=(2,0),\qquad\mu_2=(2,2),\qquad J=4+4+4+4=16.$$
(c) Which of the two results would you keep? What does this tell you about $k$-means?
Solution. The first one, since $J=4<16$.
Both runs stopped, so both results are fixed points of $k$-means.
$J$ is not convex, and $k$-means converges to a local minimum that depends on the initialization.
In practice we run $k$-means from several initializations and keep the result with the smallest $J$.

2. Why k-means stops

(a) Fix the assignments $c$. Show that the $\mu_j$ that minimizes $\sum_{i:\,c^{(i)}=j}\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2$ is the mean of the points in cluster $j$.
Solution. Let $C_j=\{i: c^{(i)}=j\}$. The function is a convex quadratic in $\mu_j$, so it is minimal where its gradient vanishes: $$\nabla_{\mu_j}\sum_{i\in C_j}\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2=-2\sum_{i\in C_j}\big(x^{(i)}-\mu_j\big).$$
It vanishes when $$\sum_{i\in C_j}\big(x^{(i)}-\mu_j\big)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mu_j=\frac{1}{|C_j|}\sum_{i\in C_j}x^{(i)}.$$
(b) Explain why $J$ never increases, and why $k$-means stops after finitely many iterations.
Solution. The assignment step minimizes $J$ over $c$ with $\mu$ fixed: each point picks a nearest centroid.
The centroid step minimizes $J$ over $\mu$ with $c$ fixed, by (a).
So neither step can increase $J$.
If a point changes its cluster, it moves to a strictly nearer centroid (on a tie it keeps its cluster), so $J$ strictly decreases. Hence no assignment $c$ can occur twice.
There are at most $k^n$ assignments, so after finitely many iterations $c$ stops changing.

3. Mixture of Gaussians versus GDA

(a) What is the main difference between a mixture of Gaussians and Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (GDA)?
Solution. Both models generate a point in two steps: a label $j$ with probability $\phi_j$, then $x\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j,\Sigma_j)$.
In GDA the label $y^{(i)}$ of every training point is observed: it is a supervised model.
In a mixture of Gaussians the label $z^{(i)}$ is latent: it is an unsupervised model, and the parameters have to be estimated without knowing which Gaussian generated each point.
(b) Suppose the labels $z^{(i)}$ were known. How would you estimate $\phi_j$, $\mu_j$ and $\Sigma_j$?
Solution. Then maximum likelihood is the same as for GDA: we fit one Gaussian to the points of each component. $$\phi_j=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\},$$
$$\mu_j=\frac{\sum_i\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}\,x^{(i)}}{\sum_i\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}},$$
$$\Sigma_j=\frac{\sum_i\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}\,(x^{(i)}-\mu_j)(x^{(i)}-\mu_j)^\top}{\sum_i\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}}.$$ These are the fraction, the mean and the covariance of the points with $z^{(i)}=j$.
EM replaces the unknown indicators $\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}$ by the responsibilities $w_j^{(i)}=p(z^{(i)}=j\mid x^{(i)})$ (E-step) and then applies these formulas (M-step).

4. EM in one dimension

We fit a mixture of two Gaussians to the four points $x^{(1)}=0$, $x^{(2)}=1$, $x^{(3)}=3$, $x^{(4)}=4$. Both Gaussians have variance $\sigma^2=1$, which is kept fixed. We initialize $\phi_1=\phi_2=\tfrac12$, $\mu_1=1$, $\mu_2=3$ and perform one iteration of EM.
(a) E-step. Compute the responsibilities $w_1^{(i)}=p(z^{(i)}=1\mid x^{(i)})$. Hint: show first that here $w_1^{(i)}=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{\,2x^{(i)}-4}}$.
Solution. By Bayes' rule, $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{\phi_1\,\mathcal N(x^{(i)};\mu_1,1)}{\phi_1\,\mathcal N(x^{(i)};\mu_1,1)+\phi_2\,\mathcal N(x^{(i)};\mu_2,1)}.$$
Since $\phi_1=\phi_2$, the factors $\phi_j/\sqrt{2\pi}$ cancel: $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{e^{-(x^{(i)}-1)^2/2}}{e^{-(x^{(i)}-1)^2/2}+e^{-(x^{(i)}-3)^2/2}}.$$
Dividing numerator and denominator by $e^{-(x^{(i)}-1)^2/2}$, $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{1}{1+\exp\!\Big(\tfrac12\big[(x^{(i)}-1)^2-(x^{(i)}-3)^2\big]\Big)}.$$
Since $(x-1)^2-(x-3)^2=4x-8$, the exponent is $2x^{(i)}-4$. This gives

$w_1^{(i)}$0.9820.8810.1190.018
$w_2^{(i)}=1-w_1^{(i)}$0.0180.1190.8810.982
All responsibilities lie strictly between 0 and 1: unlike in $k$-means, the assignments are soft.
(b) M-step. Compute the updated $\phi_1$, $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$.
Solution. We use the formulas of Problem 3(b), with $\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}$ replaced by $w_j^{(i)}$: $$\phi_1=\tfrac14\textstyle\sum_i w_1^{(i)}=\tfrac14(0.982+0.881+0.119+0.018)=\tfrac24=0.5,$$
$$\mu_1=\frac{\sum_i w_1^{(i)}x^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_1^{(i)}}=\frac{0+0.881+3\cdot0.119+4\cdot0.018}{2}=\frac{1.310}{2}\approx0.66,$$
$$\mu_2=\frac{\sum_i w_2^{(i)}x^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_2^{(i)}}=\frac{0.119+3\cdot0.881+4\cdot0.982}{2}=\frac{6.690}{2}\approx3.34.$$
(c) How did the means change? Explain the change using the data.
Solution. They moved apart, from $1$ and $3$ to $0.66$ and $3.34$. The data form two clusters, $\{0,1\}$ with mean $0.5$ and $\{3,4\}$ with mean $3.5$, and each mean moves towards the cluster whose points have a large responsibility for it.
The log-likelihood increases from $-7.16$ to $-6.86$.
(d) Without computing anything: what is the responsibility $w_1$ of an additional point $x=2$ under the initial parameters?
Solution. $w_1=\tfrac12$. The point is equidistant from $\mu_1=1$ and $\mu_2=3$, and both Gaussians have the same weight and variance, so the two terms in Bayes' rule are equal. (Check: $1/(1+e^{0})=\tfrac12$.)
(e) Run $k$-means on the same four points, starting from $\mu_1=1$ and $\mu_2=3$. Compare its means with those of EM.
Solution. The assignment step puts $0,1$ in cluster $1$ and $3,4$ in cluster $2$, so $\mu_1=0.5$ and $\mu_2=3.5$.
The next assignment step changes nothing, and $k$-means stops.
EM instead converges to $\mu_1\approx0.56$ and $\mu_2\approx3.44$.
Its assignments are soft: the points $1$ and $3$ keep a responsibility of about $0.05$ for the other Gaussian and pull the means slightly towards each other.
$k$-means is the hard version, in which every $w_j^{(i)}$ is $0$ or $1$.
