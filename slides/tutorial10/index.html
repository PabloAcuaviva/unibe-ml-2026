1. k-means by hand

$$x^{(1)}=(0,0),\quad x^{(2)}=(0,2),\quad x^{(3)}=(4,0),\quad x^{(4)}=(4,2)$$ $k=2$ clusters, distortion $J(c,\mu)=\sum_{i=1}^n\|x^{(i)}-\mu_{c^{(i)}}\|^2$.
(a) Initialize $\mu_1=x^{(1)}$ and $\mu_2=x^{(3)}$. Run $k$-means until the assignments no longer change. Give the centroids and $J$.
Solution. Assignment step: $c^{(i)}=\arg\min_j\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2$
Centroid step: $\mu_j=$ mean of $\{x^{(i)}: c^{(i)}=j\}$
Assignment step:
$c$ unchanged $\Rightarrow$ stop.
$$\mu_1=(0,1),\qquad\mu_2=(4,1),\qquad J=1+1+1+1=4.$$
(b) Now initialize $\mu_1=x^{(1)}$ and $\mu_2=x^{(2)}$ and run $k$-means again.
Solution. Assignment step:
Centroid step: $$\begin{aligned}\mu_1&=\tfrac12\big(x^{(1)}+x^{(3)}\big)=\tfrac12\big((0,0)+(4,0)\big)=(2,0),\\\mu_2&=\tfrac12\big(x^{(2)}+x^{(4)}\big)=\tfrac12\big((0,2)+(4,2)\big)=(2,2).\end{aligned}$$
Assignment step:
$c$ unchanged $\Rightarrow$ stop. $$\mu_1=(2,0),\qquad\mu_2=(2,2),\qquad J=4+4+4+4=16.$$
(c) Which of the two results would you keep? What does this tell you about $k$-means?
Solution. (a), since $J=4<16$.
Both are fixed points of $k$-means.
$J$ is not convex $\Rightarrow$ local minimum, depending on the initialization.
In practice: several initializations, keep the smallest $J$.

2. Why k-means stops

(a) Fix the assignments $c$. Show that the $\mu_j$ that minimizes $\sum_{i:\,c^{(i)}=j}\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2$ is the mean of the points in cluster $j$.
Solution. $C_j=\{i: c^{(i)}=j\}$. Convex in $\mu_j$ $\Rightarrow$ set the gradient to $0$: $$\nabla_{\mu_j}\sum_{i\in C_j}\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2=-2\sum_{i\in C_j}\big(x^{(i)}-\mu_j\big).$$
$$\sum_{i\in C_j}\big(x^{(i)}-\mu_j\big)=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mu_j=\frac{1}{|C_j|}\sum_{i\in C_j}x^{(i)}.$$
(b) Explain why $J$ never increases, and why $k$-means stops after finitely many iterations.
Solution. Assignment step: minimizes $J$ over $c$ ($\mu$ fixed). $$\min_{c}\,J(c,\mu)\quad\Rightarrow\quad c^{(i)}=\arg\min_j\|x^{(i)}-\mu_j\|^2$$
Centroid step: minimizes $J$ over $\mu$ ($c$ fixed), by (a). $$\min_{\mu}\,J(c,\mu)\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mu_j=\text{mean of }\{x^{(i)}: c^{(i)}=j\}$$
$\Rightarrow$ $J$ never increases.
$c$ changes only to a strictly nearer centroid (tie: keep) $\Rightarrow$ $J$ strictly decreases $\Rightarrow$ no $c$ occurs twice.
At most $k^n$ assignments $\Rightarrow$ $c$ stops changing after finitely many iterations.

3. Mixture of Gaussians versus GDA

(a) What is the main difference between a mixture of Gaussians and Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (GDA)?
Solution. Both: label $j$ with probability $\phi_j$, then $x\sim\mathcal N(\mu_j,\Sigma_j)$.
GDA: $y^{(i)}$ observed $\Rightarrow$ supervised.
Mixture: $z^{(i)}$ latent $\Rightarrow$ unsupervised.
(b) Suppose the labels $z^{(i)}$ were known. How would you estimate $\phi_j$, $\mu_j$ and $\Sigma_j$?
Solution. Maximum likelihood, as in GDA: $$\phi_j=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\swapw,$$
$$\mu_j=\frac{\sum_i\swapw\,x^{(i)}}{\sum_i\swapw},$$
$$\Sigma_j=\frac{\sum_i\swapw\,(x^{(i)}-\mu_j)(x^{(i)}-\mu_j)^\top}{\sum_i\swapw}.$$
EM, labels unknown. E-step: soft labels $${\color{#c98a00}w_j^{(i)}}=p(z^{(i)}=j\mid x^{(i)})\in[0,1]$$
M-step: the formulas above, with $\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}$ replaced by ${\color{#c98a00}w_j^{(i)}}$.

4. EM in one dimension

Two Gaussians, $\sigma^2=1$ fixed, one iteration of EM.
Data: $x^{(1)}=0$, $x^{(2)}=1$, $x^{(3)}=3$, $x^{(4)}=4$.
Start: $\phi_1=\phi_2=\tfrac12$, $\mu_1=1$, $\mu_2=3$.
$x^{(i)}$0134
$w_1^{(i)}$0.9820.8810.1190.018
$w_2^{(i)}=1-w_1^{(i)}$0.0180.1190.8810.982
$0<w_j^{(i)}<1$: soft assignments.
(a) E-step. Compute the responsibilities $w_1^{(i)}=p(z^{(i)}=1\mid x^{(i)})$. Hint: show first that here $w_1^{(i)}=\dfrac{1}{1+e^{\,2x^{(i)}-4}}$.
Solution. Model, for each point $i$ and $j\in\{1,2\}$: $$\underbrace{P(z^{(i)}=j)}_{\text{prior}}=\phi_j,\qquad\underbrace{p(x^{(i)}\mid z^{(i)}=j)}_{\text{likelihood}}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-\mu_j)^2/2}$$
Bayes: $$\begin{aligned}w_1^{(i)}&=P(z^{(i)}=1\mid x^{(i)})\\&=\frac{P(z^{(i)}=1)\,p(x^{(i)}\mid z^{(i)}=1)}{P(z^{(i)}=1)\,p(x^{(i)}\mid z^{(i)}=1)+P(z^{(i)}=2)\,p(x^{(i)}\mid z^{(i)}=2)}\end{aligned}$$
Insert the model: $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{\phi_1\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-\mu_1)^2/2}}{\phi_1\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-\mu_1)^2/2}+\phi_2\,\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-\mu_2)^2/2}}$$
Plug in $\phi_1=\phi_2=\tfrac12$, $\mu_1=1$, $\mu_2=3$: $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{{\color{#8c8c8c}\cancel{\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-1)^2/2}}{{\color{#8c8c8c}\cancel{\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-1)^2/2}+{\color{#8c8c8c}\cancel{\tfrac12\cdot\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}}}\,e^{-(x^{(i)}-3)^2/2}}$$
Divide by $e^{-(x^{(i)}-1)^2/2}$: $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\left[(x^{(i)}-1)^2-(x^{(i)}-3)^2\right]/2}}$$
$(x^{(i)}-1)^2-(x^{(i)}-3)^2=4x^{(i)}-8$: $$w_1^{(i)}=\frac{1}{1+e^{\,2x^{(i)}-4}}$$
(b) M-step. Compute the updated $\phi_1$, $\mu_1$ and $\mu_2$.
Solution. Recall 3(b):
$$\phi_j=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\swapw,\qquad\mu_j=\frac{\sum_i\swapw\,x^{(i)}}{\sum_i\swapw}$$
$\mathbf 1\{z^{(i)}=j\}$ replaced by ${\color{#c98a00}w_j^{(i)}}$
$$\phi_1=\tfrac14\textstyle\sum_i w_1^{(i)}=\tfrac14(0.982+0.881+0.119+0.018)=\tfrac24=0.5,$$
$$\mu_1=\frac{\sum_i w_1^{(i)}x^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_1^{(i)}}=\frac{0+0.881+3\cdot0.119+4\cdot0.018}{2}=\frac{1.310}{2}\approx0.66,$$
$$\mu_2=\frac{\sum_i w_2^{(i)}x^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_2^{(i)}}=\frac{0.119+3\cdot0.881+4\cdot0.982}{2}=\frac{6.690}{2}\approx3.34.$$
(c) How did the means change? Explain the change using the data.
Solution. $\mu_1: 1\to0.66$, $\mu_2: 3\to3.34$: apart, towards the clusters $\{0,1\}$ (mean $0.5$) and $\{3,4\}$ (mean $3.5$).
(d) Without computing anything: what is the responsibility $w_1$ of an additional point $x=2$ under the initial parameters?
Solution. $w_1=\tfrac12$: $x=2$ is equidistant from $\mu_1=1$ and $\mu_2=3$, same $\phi_j$ and $\sigma^2$. Check: $1/(1+e^{2\cdot2-4})=\tfrac12$.
(e) Run $k$-means on the same four points, starting from $\mu_1=1$ and $\mu_2=3$. Compare its means with those of EM.
Solution. $c=(1,1,2,2)$ $\Rightarrow$ $\mu_1=0.5$, $\mu_2=3.5$.
$c$ unchanged $\Rightarrow$ stop.
EM converges to $\mu_1\approx0.56$, $\mu_2\approx3.44$.
Why? Weights of Gaussian 1:
$x^{(i)}$0134
$k$-means: $w_1^{(i)}$1100
EM: $w_1^{(i)}$0.9970.9470.0530.003
$\mu_1=\dfrac{\sum_i w_1^{(i)}x^{(i)}}{\sum_i w_1^{(i)}}$, with $\sum_i w_1^{(i)}=2$ in both: $$\begin{aligned}k\text{-means:}\quad\mu_1&=\frac{1\cdot0+1\cdot1+0\cdot3+0\cdot4}{2}=0.5\\[4pt]\text{EM:}\quad\mu_1&=\frac{0.997\cdot0+0.947\cdot1+{\color{#c98a00}0.053\cdot3+0.003\cdot4}}{2}\approx0.56\end{aligned}$$
${\color{#c98a00}x=3,4}$ still pull $\mu_1$ to the right; $x=0,1$ pull $\mu_2$ to the left $\Rightarrow$ means move towards each other.
$k$-means = EM with every $w_j^{(i)}\in\{0,1\}$ (hard).

5. Jensen's inequality

Jensen: $f$ convex $\Rightarrow$ $\mathbb E[f(X)]\ge f(\mathbb E[X])$; $f$ concave $\Rightarrow$ $\le$.
$X\in\{1,4\}$, each with probability $\tfrac12$.
(a) Compute $\mathbb E[X^2]$ and $(\mathbb E[X])^2$. Which is larger?
Solution. $$\mathbb E[X]=\tfrac12\cdot1+\tfrac12\cdot4=\tfrac52,$$ $$(\mathbb E[X])^2=\tfrac{25}{4}=6.25.$$
$$\mathbb E[X^2]=\tfrac12\cdot1^2+\tfrac12\cdot4^2=\tfrac{17}{2}=8.5.$$
$\mathbb E[X^2]\ge(\mathbb E[X])^2$: $x^2$ is convex.
$\mathbb E[f(X)]$ = midpoint of the chord; convex $\Rightarrow$ chord above the graph.
(b) Compute $\mathbb E[\log X]$ and $\log\mathbb E[X]$. Which is larger?
Solution. $$\log\mathbb E[X]=\log\tfrac52\approx0.92.$$
$$\mathbb E[\log X]=\tfrac12\log1+\tfrac12\log4=\log2\approx0.69.$$
$\mathbb E[\log X]\le\log\mathbb E[X]$.
$(\log x)''=-1/x^2<0$ $\Rightarrow$ concave $\Rightarrow$ chord below the graph.
(c) When does equality hold in (a) and (b)?
Solution. (a): $\mathbb E[X^2]-(\mathbb E[X])^2=\operatorname{Var}(X)=0$ $\iff$ $X$ constant.
(b): tangent to $\log$ at any $m>0$, slope $\log'(m)=1/m$: $$t_m(x)=\log m+\frac{x-m}{m}$$
$\log$ strictly concave $\Rightarrow$ below every tangent: $$\log x\le t_m(x)\quad\text{for all }x>0,\qquad\text{equality}\iff x=m.$$
Choose $m=\mathbb E[X]$, put $x=X$: $$\log X\le\log\mathbb E[X]+\frac{X-\mathbb E[X]}{\mathbb E[X]}$$
Take $\mathbb E$: $$\mathbb E[\log X]\le\log\mathbb E[X]+\frac{\mathbb E[X]-\mathbb E[X]}{\mathbb E[X]}=\log\mathbb E[X].$$
Equality $\iff$ $\log X=t_m(X)$ $\iff$ $X=\mathbb E[X]$, with probability $1$.
